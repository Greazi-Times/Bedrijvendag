# Legitimate Interest Assessments (internal drafts)

Drafts for the organisation to complete and sign off. A conclusion below is a proposal, not a decision. If an assessment fails, another basis or a change to the processing is needed.

## 1. Public company profiles and the participant list
- **Interest:** inform students which companies take part and what they do.
- **Necessary:** a public profile cannot be informed by less. Only business information is shown; contact persons are not.
- **Individuals affected:** mainly legal entities; sole traders and owner-managers whose name or trade name identifies them.
- **Expectation:** a company that enrols expects to be listed. A sole trader may be surprised by an archive listing.
- **Impact:** low (professional context, public business information).
- **Safeguards:** no private contact data public; profile changes reviewed; objection/removal via privacy@.
- **Proposed conclusion:** passes for companies; for sole traders offer a clear objection route and review.
- **Open:** organisation decision; confirm no sensitive data in descriptions.

## 2. Historical participation and stand allocations (archive)
- **Interest:** reliable record of past editions, useful to students and for event continuity.
- **Necessary:** only edition, company, status and stand are kept; contact data and answers are purged.
- **Expectation:** reasonable for companies; less so for a sole trader after many years.
- **Impact:** low, but grows with time.
- **Safeguards:** periodic (at least yearly) review; objection and erasure rights; purge of personal contacts; no sensitive data.
- **Proposed conclusion:** passes with periodic review. Indefinite retention is conditional on continued need, not an absolute right.
- **Open:** decide the review owner and whether sole-trader entries are anonymised after N years.

## 3. Website security, security logs, fraud prevention
- **Interest:** protect accounts and systems, investigate incidents, find errors.
- **Necessary:** IP addresses and timestamps are needed to detect brute force and abuse; art. 32 GDPR expects it.
- **Expectation:** normal for a login system.
- **Impact:** low; logs are access-restricted and kept 90 days; identifiers are masked or hashed in the audit log where implemented.
- **Safeguards:** retention limit, redaction (no passwords, tokens, full invitation links, keys).
- **Proposed conclusion:** passes.
- **Open:** web-server and OS log retention and access (infrastructure).

## 4. Enrolment communication with company contacts
- **Interest:** organise participation with the represented company.
- **Alternative basis:** contract steps for sole traders.
- **Safeguards:** contact data purged after the event; only transactional mail.
- **Proposed conclusion:** passes. **Open:** per case confirmation of the basis.

## 5. Event photography (see also the procedure below)
| Image type | Proposed basis | Remarks |
|---|---|---|
| Atmosphere/overview, nobody central | legitimate interest (documentation) | notices posted; objections respected |
| Group photos | legitimate interest if the group is small and expectation is clear, otherwise consent | judge per photo before publishing |
| Identifiable close-ups | consent or a documented balancing test; default to avoid if objected | review before publication |
| Individual portraits | consent | none is registered today: a consent mechanism must be created before publishing portraits |
| Promotional use, website albums, social media | consent for recognisable people unless the test clearly passes | publish only after review |

A notice does not equal consent. **Practical procedure (to adopt):** notices at entrance and key areas, stating who to tell and the contact address; a named contact on site who relays objections to the photographers; photographers avoid anyone who objected; recognisable images reviewed before publication; after the event privacy@ handles objections within one month by removal, non-publication or blurring; check photographers' copyright/licence terms; no account needed to object. **Open:** who photographs (staff, volunteers or a contractor), where images are published, and whether the photographer is a processor.

## 6. Dietary information (not a legitimate-interest case)
Health-related answers need an Art. 9 exception in addition to an Art. 6 basis. Legitimate interest is not enough for Art. 9. Explicit consent of the participant is the realistic exception but a company representative cannot give it for a colleague. Options: ask the participant directly; reduce the form to what the caterer needs without health detail ("allergy yes/no, contact the organiser"); or collect only dietary preferences that are not health data. Until decided, the data-collection form is a review item.
